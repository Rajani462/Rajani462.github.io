PTSTORM Project

Extreme Precipitation Response to Temperature Variations: The Role of Storm Types and Global Circulation Patterns
MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship | 2025–2027
Department of Geosciences, University of Padova

The overarching objective of PTSTORM is to improve our understanding of the relation between extreme precipitation and atmospheric temperature. Current approaches often rely on scaling relations that mimic the exponential increase of water vapor availability with temperature predicted by the Clausius–Clapeyron equation ($e \propto T$).

PTSTORM provides a more realistic representation by explicitly considering the dependence on generating precipitation processes and Large-scale Atmospheric Circulation (LAC) patterns. Key objectives include:

  • Global Classification: Identifying storm types and associated precipitation characteristics.
  • Scaling Analysis: Quantifying temperature–precipitation scaling across diverse regions.
  • Circulation Modulation: Assessing how large-scale regimes influence extremes.

Methodology

We leverage high-resolution satellite products (e.g., GPM IMERG), global reanalysis datasets, and advanced statistical modeling to bridge the gap between theoretical scaling and observed physical processes.
